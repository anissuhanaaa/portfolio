Bitcoin Project: Frequency Push and ECO

Final team project (team of six), PSDC Physical Design Program

My team took a Bitcoin-inspired design (serial-to-parallel conversion, SRAM and a simplified SHA-256 block) through physical implementation in Fusion Compiler. We started from a 1 GHz baseline, ran frequency sweeps, and shortlisted three targets: 1.45, 1.55 and 1.63 GHz. After manual ECO, 1.55 GHz met its target. We also did an RTL ECO and used Formality to check that the design stayed logically equivalent. My part was frequency and recipe optimisation.

Baseline 1.00 GHz, final selected frequency 1.55 GHz with the target met after manual ECO.

ANN Diagnostic Model for Watermelon Ripeness

Final Year Project, Jul 2026

Checking watermelon ripeness by cutting it or tapping it is slow and depends on the person. I built an Artificial Neural Network in MATLAB that grades yellow flesh watermelon ripeness (grade A or B) from Visible (751 nm) and Near Infrared (1058 nm) reflectance data. I compared three training functions (traingd, traingdm and traingda) across 14 hidden neuron settings, and judged them with sensitivity, specificity and accuracy.

Best result with traingda: 83.3% accuracy, 86.7% sensitivity, 80.0% specificity.

  • ANN
  • VIS-NIR spectroscopy

Amplifier and Filter Circuit Design

Applied Electronics Circuit (ELE526), Jul 2025

I designed an analog chain to clean a weak 10 mV, 1 kHz signal: a 3-op-amp instrumentation amplifier, a precision rectifier, and a 5th-order Butterworth high-pass filter with 1 MHz cutoff. I simulated everything in LTspice and compared two op-amps. The RH1028 gave a clearer waveform and better cutoff than the LM741.

Instrumentation amplifier gain of around 100 with accurate rectification.
